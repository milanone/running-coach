# running-coach

Analyzer (Tkinter window) for running sessions exported from [intervals.icu](https://intervals.icu).
It reads the activity CSV, computes a few training-load metrics, and keeps a local history so
each new session can be compared with the previous ones.

## Metrics

- **Efficiency Factor (EF)** — metres covered per heartbeat-per-minute (`distance_m / (avg_hr × minutes)`); higher means better aerobic efficiency.
- **Cardiac drift** — how much average heart rate rises from the first to the second half of the
  steady part of the run (%). The first 10 minutes (`WARMUP_S`) and an auto-detected final-km
  sprint are excluded. A high value points to fatigue, heat or dehydration.
- **TSS** — a training-stress score from duration and heart rate relative to threshold.
- **Altitude** — the `altitude` column as recorded (minimum and maximum of the run, change over each km), read as saved: no filter, no ascent total.

### Advanced metrics (estimates)

Computed by `run_metrics.py` from the per-second data and the history, in the spirit of what a Garmin watch reports. **They are estimates:
the vendors' algorithms are proprietary, so do not expect their numbers; read the trends.** Your maximum and resting heart rate and your
mass go in `athlete.json` in the data folder (defaults 190 bpm, 60 bpm, 70 kg), never in this repository.

- **Per run**: moving time; time in the five heart-rate zones (percent of the maximum, as Garmin's defaults) and the Edwards TRIMP;
  a 0-5 *aerobic training effect* and a *recovery time* (both **indicative**: the scale is mine, not calibrated against a Garmin);
  **VO2max estimate** (heart rate at a steady speed, ACSM running equation, heart-rate reserve taken as VO2 reserve: about +-10%);
  **pace at a fixed heart rate** and **heart rate at the usual speed** (the cleanest fitness trend: it does not depend on how hard you chose to run);
  steps per minute and step length (a CSV cadence below 120 is read as per foot and doubled); a **rough power model**
  (not a measured power); **performance condition** (efficiency of minutes 6-20 against your last ten runs); heart-rate recovery in 60 s
  (when the recording goes on after you stop) and heart-rate rise time at the start; second half against first half, kilometre-time variability;
  best efforts (fastest 1 km, 1 mile, 3, 5, 10 km, half marathon inside the run) and surges/intervals detected from the speed.
- **From the history**: *Fitness* (CTL, 42 days), *Fatigue* (ATL, 7 days) and *Form* (TSB) from the TSS; the acute/chronic load ratio
  (load of the last 7 days over the weekly average of the last 28, as Garmin states it); a training status label (a heuristic modelled on Garmin's:
  Productive, Maintaining, Recovery, Unproductive, Ramping up, Strained, Overreaching, Detraining); race predictions from the VO2max
  (Daniels-Gilbert VDOT) and from your best recent effort (Riegel, slow because training is not racing); all-time best efforts.
- Not possible with this data: ground contact time, vertical oscillation, HRV, Body Battery, sleep (they need other sensors).

**Interval workouts.** A run with repetitions and recoveries has no steady effort, so the numbers made for a steady one (drift, efficiency,
VO2max, pace at a heart rate) would be wrong for it: the heart rate of a repetition is climbing and the recovery
drags the average down. The program recognises such a session from the speed alone: two or more fast stretches of at least 30 s, each pair
separated by a recovery (30 s to 10 min) that is clearly slower, taking at most 70% of the run. It then marks the row `intervals` in the
history, leaves the drift and the efficiency empty, computes the TSS second by second (the square of the intensity factor averaged over the
seconds, not of the average heart rate), lists the repetitions (time, distance, pace, heart rate, the heart-rate drop in the next 60 s, and the
change from the first to the last) in the Fitness tab, and starts the advice for the next run from your last steady run (one more rest day, no
progression). The zones, the TRIMP, the training effect and the best efforts are still computed, and two numbers take a form that suits
repetitions: the **heart-rate recovery** is the mean drop from the peak to 60 s after the end of each repetition, and the **performance
condition** compares the efficiency of the repetitions with that of your earlier interval workouts (it needs at least three, and the
workouts have to be of a similar structure to be comparable). It is a heuristic: a run with a long walking
break could be taken for one, and a workout with very short or irregular repetitions may not be recognised. Of the 40 runs it was tried on, only the two
real interval sessions were marked. Two recordings of one workout can be joined with *Merge runs*.

The values are computed in a thread when the **Fitness** tab, or **Charts** on an advanced metric, is opened, and cached in
`run_metrics_cache.json` in the data folder (a run is recomputed only when its CSV, a recording merged into it, its FIT file or the athlete parameters change; a new run or a merge elsewhere
recomputes nothing). The first time, or after an update that changes a formula, every run is read once (about two seconds for 40 runs, and the tab shows the
progress); the **Recompute all metrics** button of the Fitness tab does it on request.

**A heart rate clipped at a ceiling.** intervals.icu treats a heart rate above the maximum set for the sport as a spike and cuts it to
that maximum: if the *Run* maximum heart rate in Settings (Heart Rate Training Zones) is lower than your real one, every hard stretch shows
as a flat line at that value in the stream, in the chart and in the CSV, while the watch and the FIT file written by Health Sync have the
real numbers. A change of that setting does not repair the activities already cut: reprocess them (the activity's Actions menu, *Update
zones or reprocess original file*) and download the CSV again. Two protections here:

- **Health Sync folder (FIT files, optional)**: when the box at the top of the window points to the folder with the original `.fit` files
  (the same name as the CSV, `YYYY.MM.DD HH.MM-RUNNING.fit`), the heart rate of a run is read from the FIT instead of the CSV when the CSV looks clipped (20 seconds or more exactly at its maximum;
  otherwise the FIT is not even opened), second by second (the mean of the two records per second). The FIT is used only if its heart rate agrees with the CSV's where the CSV is not clipped
  (mean difference up to 3 bpm), so a file of another run is ignored. It needs the optional `fitparse` package (`py -m pip install fitparse`);
  without it, or without the file, the CSV is used. The History columns (HR, EF, drift, TSS) change with *Reprocess all*; the Fitness
  metrics and the run detail change at once. The environment variable `RUNNING_COACH_FIT` sets the same folder.
- **Clipped plateaus are left out**: 30 s or more at exactly the maximum heart rate of the run is not a heart rate (a real peak moves by a
  beat or two). Those seconds are dropped from the metrics (VO2max, pace at a fixed heart rate, efficiency, zones: the TRIMP and the
  training effect are then a minimum) and the run detail says so.

### Sources and how far each estimate is checked

None of the estimates is validated against a Garmin watch or a laboratory. This is what each rests on, as of October 2026.

**Sources whose text or abstract was read**

| Used for | Source | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| VO2max from the heart rate | Swain and Leutholtz, 1997, *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise* 29:410-4, [doi:10.1097/00005768-199703000-00018](https://doi.org/10.1097/00005768-199703000-00018) | In 63 adults, the percentage of heart-rate reserve equals the percentage of VO2 reserve (slope 1.00, intercept -0.1), not of VO2max. Measured on a cycle ergometer, not in running. |
| Rough running power | Minetti et al., 2002, *Journal of Applied Physiology* 93:1039-46, [doi:10.1152/japplphysiol.01177.2001](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.01177.2001) | The metabolic cost of running is 3.40 J/kg/m on the level, independent of speed, and changes with the slope. The coefficient 1.04 W per kg per m/s is 0.31 of that: the 0.31 (mechanical over metabolic) is an assumption, not a measured efficiency. |
| VO2max method and its accuracy | Firstbeat, [Automated Fitness Level (VO2max) Estimation with Heart Rate and Speed Data](https://www.firstbeat.com/en/aerobic-fitness-level-vo%e2%82%82max-estimation-firstbeat-white-paper-2/), 2014, updated 2017 | The same idea (heart rate against speed, in reliable stretches only, with the maximum heart rate). Their algorithm has an error of about 5% against the laboratory over 2690 runs, and about 7-9% if the maximum heart rate is off by 15 bpm. That accuracy does **not** transfer to the simplified version here. |
| Training effect scale | Firstbeat, [EPOC Based Training Effect Assessment](https://www.firstbeat.com/en/training-effect-firstbeat-white-paper-2/), 2005, updated 2012 | The 0-5 bands. Their value comes from the peak EPOC and the athlete's activity class of the last month; the training effect here is **not** that method (see below). |
| Load ratio bands | Garmin manual, [Training Status](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals-apac/webhelp/marqathletegen2/EN-SG/GUID-9C1771B0-C377-4A7D-B010-548D96ADB9D4-8721.html) | Load ratio: low below 0.8, optimal 0.8 to 1.4, high 1.5 to 1.9, very high 2.0 or more. The status labels are only described there, without thresholds. |

**Sources known only through secondary descriptions (the primary text was not read)**

- *Race predictions from the best effort*: Riegel, "Athletic Records and Human Endurance", *American Scientist* 69(3):285-290, 1981 ([JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/27850427)). The exponent 1.06 is the popular rounding; a reconstruction of Riegel's own regression gives about 1.077, and the formula is said to work best between 1.5 km and the marathon.
- *VO2 at a steady speed*: the ACSM running equation `0.2 x speed + 0.9 x speed x grade + 3.5` (speed in m/min), from the ACSM Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription. It is described as valid above 134 m/min (8 km/h), or from 80 m/min when truly jogging. A [study of athletes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24376318/) found it 14.6% above the measured VO2max, but that used it to predict a maximal test, not as here.
- *Race predictions from the VO2max*: the Daniels-Gilbert VDOT equations (J. Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*). The constants match several online calculators and reproduce the VDOT 50 row of Daniels' table (5 km 19:56 against 19:57, 10 km 41:20 against 41:21, marathon 3:10:40 against 3:10:49).
- *TRIMP*: Edwards' zone weights 1 to 5 (S. Edwards, *The Heart Rate Monitor Book*, 1993), from memory of the book.
- *Fitness, Fatigue, Form*: the usual exponential averages of 42 and 7 days of the TSS.

**What is mine, not from a source**

- The **training effect** (`5 x (1 - exp(-TRIMP / 110))`) and the **recovery time** (`4 x TE^2` hours): only the 0-5 reading is Firstbeat's; the scale is not calibrated against a Garmin, and Firstbeat's own does not depend on the TRIMP.
- The **training status** labels (Productive, Maintaining, Recovery, Unproductive, Ramping up, Strained, Overreaching, Detraining) and their thresholds, including "Ramping up" below 100 TSS in 7 days.
- Reading the **chronic load** as the weekly average of the last 28 days (Garmin and Firstbeat define it as the load of the last 28 days).
- The **performance condition** window (minutes 6-20, against the median of the previous ten runs).

**Accuracy to expect.** On a real set of 35 easy runs the per-run VO2max estimate scatters by about +-10% between runs, so read the median of the last five and the trend. The maximum heart rate matters: Firstbeat reports 7-9% of error on the VO2max for 15 bpm of error on it, so use a measured value if you can. For slow jogging (below about 8 km/h, where the ACSM equation is at the edge of its validity) the
VO2max is less certain, and the race predictions from it are optimistic: they say what a trained runner with that VO2max would run,
not what a slow, non-racing runner will.

## GUI

```
py running-coach-gui.pyw        # or double-click the file (Windows opens it without a console)
```

Pick the data folder (the one with the `YYYY.MM.DD HH.MM-RUNNING.csv` files and `running_history.csv`;
it is remembered in `~/.running_coach_gui.json`), then:

- **Download new sessions from intervals.icu** fetches the per-second streams of the runs not yet in the folder
  (running only, saved as `YYYY.MM.DD HH.MM-RUNNING.csv`) and then imports them. It needs your intervals.icu API key
  (Settings → Developer Settings): put it in `intervals_api_key.txt` next to the program (gitignored) or in the
  `INTERVALS_API_KEY` environment variable.
- **Import local CSVs (legacy)** analyses only the CSVs already in the folder and not yet in the history (no download). **Reprocess all**
  recomputes every session (the weather already recorded is kept).
- **History** lists all sessions; select one to see the per-km splits, sprint note and weather.
- **Run detail** (double-click a run, or open the tab) plots every per-second column saved in the CSV for the selected run:
  speed (`velocity_smooth`, shown as km/h or as pace in min/km with the faster pace up), heart rate, cadence, and both `altitude` and
  `fixed_altitude`, against time or distance.
  Moving the mouse shows all the values of that second (with pace and GPS position).
- **Fitness** is laid out in four cards: the **next session** (when you are ready, distance and pace, from the TSS, the drift, the efficiency
  trend, the load ratio and your pace at a fixed heart rate, with the reasons), the **training status** (status, load ratio, fitness, fatigue,
  form), the **VO2max** estimate and the **race predictions**; under them the best efforts, then a table with one row per run and, for the
  selected run, a panel with its numbers, the time in each heart-rate zone as a bar and, for an interval workout, the list of repetitions. The
  same metrics can be plotted over time in **Charts**.
- **Gauges** puts the values that have a reference range on half-circle dials, one card each, for a run chosen at the top (the load ones are
  as of today): load ratio, form (TSB), heart-rate drift, average heart rate as a percentage of the maximum, training effect, performance
  condition, heart-rate recovery and the VO2max. The coloured bands are the reference ranges, the needle is the value, and the card says in words what
  the band means, what the value is, and where the range comes from (the text can be selected and copied): a source that was read (load ratio: Garmin's manual; training effect:
  Firstbeat; heart-rate recovery: Jou et al. 2025, a 5th percentile for your age and resting heart rate, or Cole 1999 without an age), secondary sources (drift: TrainingPeaks; form: Friel and a third-party table; VO2max: the Cooper
  Institute percentiles as the Garmin manuals reprint them) or a rule of thumb of this program (zones, performance condition). The VO2max is
  placed against the norms for your age (20-79) and sex, which you enter with the **Athlete...** button at the top (it also edits the heart
  rates and the mass); without them the dial asks for them. Values with no published range are not shown.
- **Map** draws the GPS track of the selected run (start in green, end in red) over an OpenStreetMap map of the area, with a scale bar,
  coloured by speed (slow blue to fast red, between the 5th and 95th percentile of the run, with a legend). Drag to move, mouse wheel to zoom
  (the point under the cursor stays put), **Fit** to see the whole track again; hovering the track shows time, distance, speed and pace,
  heart rate, cadence and altitude at that point.
  The tiles are downloaded from `tile.openstreetmap.org` only while this tab is open and are cached in `~/.running_coach_tiles`
  (one run needs about 10-20 tiles). The requests carry tile numbers, not your track, but they do reveal the area you are looking at.
  Without a connection the track is drawn on a grey background. The example above is a synthetic loop, not a real run.
- **Merge runs / Unmerge runs** (right-click in the History table, select two or more runs first): merges runs recorded as
  one outing; nothing is deleted, the CSVs stay and *Unmerge runs* restores them. Merged sessions are highlighted.
- **Charts** plots distance, pace, heart rate, EF, drift or TSS over time with a 3-session moving average.

The program is the GUI (`pythonw running-coach-gui.pyw`); `parse_run_csv.py` holds the logic it uses and the history format.

**Weather lookup.** For each session it queries the [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com) historical API with the **start
coordinates** of the activity to record temperature, humidity and wind. If the request fails the program continues without
weather data. Untick **Online weather** (or set `FETCH_WEATHER = False`) if you do not want coordinates sent to an external service.

## Outings recorded as several sessions

If a run was recorded as several sessions (the recording restarted after a pause), select them in the History table and use
the right-click menu **Merge runs**, or list them by hand in `merged_sessions.json`
in the data folder, the first one as the key: `{"2026.08.18 16.45": ["2026.08.18 17.21", "2026.08.18 17.38"]}`.
They are then analysed as one session starting at the first: distances and moving times add up (the pause is not
counted), and the member sessions disappear from the history. Their CSVs stay in the folder so a download does not
fetch them again. Run **Import local CSVs (legacy)** (or the download button) once to apply it.

## The history file

`running_history.csv` has one row per session with the columns `date` (dd/mm/yyyy), `time`, `dist_km`, `duration_hms`,
`pace_minkm`, `avg_hr_bpm`, `ef_m_beat`, `drift_pct`, `tss`, `temp_c`, `humidity_pct`, `wind_kmh` and `kind` (`intervals` for an interval workout,
else empty).
The recommendations use only objective data (TSS, cardiac drift, EF trend); the former subjective "legs" rating was
removed. Files written by earlier versions (Italian column names such as `data`, `orario`, and the `gambe` / `legs`
column) are still read: the names are translated and the legs column is dropped the next time the history is saved.

## Configuration

Personal parameters are constants at the top of the script. Edit them to match your own values:

| Constant | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `THRESHOLD_HR` | Lactate-threshold heart rate (bpm) |
| `EF_BASELINE` | Baseline efficiency (m/beat) used as a reference |
| `FETCH_WEATHER` | Query Open-Meteo for weather (the *Online weather* box of the GUI overrides it) |
| `DRIFT_LOW`, `DRIFT_HIGH`, `DRIFT_VERY_HIGH` | Drift thresholds (%) for the "good" / "high" / "very high" messages and the rest-day and distance adjustments |
| `WARMUP_S` | Seconds excluded from the start when computing cardiac drift |
| `SPRINT_PACE_S`, `SPRINT_HR_BPM` | How much faster / higher than the median flags a sprint |

**Keeping data out of this repo.** Point the data folder box of the GUI (or the `RUNNING_COACH_DATA`
environment variable, read at start-up) at a separate folder: the activity CSVs are read from it and
`running_history.csv` is written to it — for example a private data-only repository cloned next to this one.

Body parameters for the advanced metrics (`hr_max`, `hr_rest`, `mass_kg`, optionally `age` and `sex` (`"M"` or `"F"`, for the VO2max norms of the Gauges tab) and `height_cm` (for the typical heart-rate recovery of your age; the
**Athlete...** button writes them), `cadence_factor` 1 or 2, `hr_ref`, and `hr_max_estimated: true` when the maximum heart rate is a formula or a guess, not measured) go in
`athlete.json` in that folder, e.g. `{"hr_max": 190, "hr_rest": 60, "mass_kg": 70}`.

## Tests

```
py -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

`tests/synth.py` generates synthetic intervals.icu-style CSVs (doubled Samsung distance, GPS lockup
at the start, optional final-km sprint), so the logic can be checked without real data.

## Privacy

This repository contains code only. `.gitignore` excludes `*.csv`, `*.fit`, `*.tcx`, `*.gpx`
and `running_history.csv`, so activity exports, GPS tracks and your history never get committed.
Keep your own data files out of version control.

## Requirements

Python 3, standard library only (the charts are drawn on a Tkinter canvas, without matplotlib). The GUI needs Tkinter, which ships with the standard Python
installer on Windows and macOS (Linux: `sudo apt install python3-tk`).
